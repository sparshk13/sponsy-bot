# Email writing guide

Emails must read like a student wrote them to one person, not like a template or a model. The same applies to the app's own copy. The validator checks the mechanical parts; this guide covers the voice. The drafting prompt includes these rules.

## Shape
- Plain text only. 80 to 160 words for a first email; 40 to 100 for a follow-up.
- Subject: specific, no more than 8 words, no clickbait, no "Partnership opportunity". Examples of the pattern: "Credits for a 24-hour student hackathon", "Snacks for 150 students at our hackathon".
- Greeting by first name if the contact is known, otherwise "Hello" and the team name.
- Paragraph 1: who we are and the event, one or two sentences, with the date and audience from the campaign details.
- Paragraph 2: why this company, based on one verified fact. If there is no verified fact, say nothing specific about them.
- Paragraph 3: one clear ask, matching the tier, with the amount or quantity from the campaign details, and what the sponsor gets in return if the campaign lists it.
- Close: offer a short call or a reply with questions. Name, role, club, college.
- Last line, always: an opt-out sentence, for example "If this is not for you, tell me and I will not write again."

## Voice
- Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No flattery. One idea per paragraph.
- Never write: "I hope this email finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "excited to", "thrilled", "synergy", "leverage", "unique opportunity", "win-win", "delve", "in today's fast-paced", "at the intersection of".
- No em dashes. No emoji. No bullet lists in a first email. No bold. No exclamation marks.
- Do not mimic a company's marketing language back at them.
- Never claim anything about the company that is not a verified fact. Never invent numbers, attendance, or past results for our own club either.

## Follow-ups
- First follow-up: two or three sentences, one new detail (a speaker, a prize track, a deadline), same ask in one line.
- Second follow-up: closes the loop. Say this is the last note, repeat the ask once, thank them either way.

## Replying to interest
Thank them by name, give the calendar link, suggest that a 20 minute call is enough, and say what we will bring to it. No pressure, no extra attachments.

## Pattern examples (style only; placeholders are filled from campaign details and verified facts)
First email, credits tier:
> Subject: Credits for a 24-hour student hackathon
>
> Hello [first name],
>
> I organise [event name] at [college], a 24-hour hackathon on [date] for about [audience] students.
>
> I am writing to you because [one verified fact about the company, in plain words].
>
> Could [company] provide [credits ask from campaign] for participants? In return we can [campaign benefit]. If a short call is easier, I can do any weekday afternoon.
>
> [Name], [role], [club], [college]
> If this is not for you, tell me and I will not write again.

Follow-up 2:
> Subject: Last note about [event name]
>
> Hello [first name], this is my last message about [event name] on [date]. The ask is still [short ask]. Whatever you decide, thank you for reading.
>
> [Name], [club]
> If this is not for you, tell me and I will not write again.
